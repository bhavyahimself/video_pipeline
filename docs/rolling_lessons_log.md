# Rolling Lessons Log

## 2026-07-15

- Nature Unleashed: literal object-story hooks are stronger than poetic science framing when the footage itself is the payoff.
- One Decision: strong body retention does not rescue soft packaging. If the title is too descriptive, distribution can die before the viewer gets the story.
- Designed To Trick You: concept videos fail when the trap is abstract in frame one. Show the manipulation before explaining it.
- Why This Place Failed: named-company failures are stronger than category commentary when the mechanism can be isolated in one sentence.
- What Your X Says: universal daily objects outperform narrower lifestyle markers on identity-pull channels.
- VEED workflow: when reusing one project for multiple TTS exports, download each render immediately. Later generations can invalidate earlier signed export URLs.

## 2026-07-19

- Designed To Trick You: technically polished generic shopping footage still failed because it did not prove the narrated mechanism. Exact fictional interface animation for card entry, renewal, statement charge, cancellation friction, and reminders made the story understandable without relying on captions alone.
- All channels: narration-to-visual alignment is now a release-blocking requirement, not a subjective polish step. Every beat needs a written visual meaning, an exact asset, and a match-confidence judgment.
- Growth system: evaluate uploads at comparable ages and normalize engagement and subscriber gains per 1,000 views. Raw lifetime views alone are not a useful daily learning signal.
- Experiment discipline: select concepts from a scored candidate pool and test one primary creative variable per upload when possible.

## 2026-07-20

- Yesterday's winner was **The Town Burning Underneath**: 1,032 views, 59.3% stayed to watch, 43.7% average percentage viewed, and 17 seconds average view duration on a 39-second Short. A concrete place plus an impossible condition beat abstract or generic premises.
- The batch-wide failure was the opening second. Four of five Shorts stayed below 42% stayed-to-watch; **What Your Alarm Habit Says About You** fell to 6.4%. Frame one must identify the exact subject, show movement, and create one instantly understood contradiction.
- Payoffs were arriving after the attention already disappeared. Until retention improves, default to 28–35 seconds, reveal at roughly 65–75% of runtime, then use one consequence or loop beat.
- Do not overfit tiny samples. **Why Free Trials Ask for Your Card** had one view, so it is a distribution/classification test—not reliable evidence about the full script.
- **One Decision / Cuban Missile Crisis was rejected at human review.** The script's decision structure was acceptable, but eight diagram/map scenes looked lower quality than the approved batch. Maps can explain geography, but they cannot carry an entire cinematic history Short.
- One Decision visual gate: use real licensed or public-domain archival motion for people, rooms, aircraft, ships, and consequences; use animated maps only for the exact spatial beat; add period-accurate dates and labels; and require shot-to-line relevance plus perceived-production-quality review before release.
- The path toward 1M views is a repeatable experiment program, not a metadata trick: test topic strength, first-frame clarity, and hook wording first; then pacing and payoff timing. Promote a format only after it wins at comparable ages across at least three uploads.
- Operating targets for the next phase: 80%+ stayed-to-watch and 80%+ average percentage viewed on 30–60 second Shorts, alongside rising rewatches/shares and a clear subscriber conversion signal. A video must beat the channel median before its structure is reused.
- Scale rule: when a Short materially outperforms the channel median, produce 3–5 adjacent variations of the same viewer promise within seven days while changing the story—not merely reposting the same edit.
- User-set stretch target: future 30–60 second Shorts should pursue 80%+ stayed-to-watch and 80%+ average percentage viewed. The first six seconds must now show literal proof, ask a personally important question, complicate the obvious answer, and deliver an early evidence reward.
- **Cuban Missile Crisis recut:** retain a script and voice that passed review, but replace weak visual language independently. The approved correction uses 22 public-domain archival shots, a viewer-choice hook ("Would you strike first?"), literal proof in the opening seconds, and a 1.97-second average visual reset. This becomes the One Decision release gate: no all-map history Shorts when human action and consequences are available on film.

## 2026-07-30

- **Visual improvement for future gameplay and explainers:** captions and diagrams cannot substitute for the exact subject in motion. Roblox and game stories must use owned or licensed capture of the named experience, mechanic, UI action, consequence, and payoff. Real-world explainers should use authentic footage for named people, places, objects, and events, reserving diagrams for invisible mechanisms or spatial relationships.
- **Retention hypothesis:** a footage-first sequence of consequence → decision close-up → UI/evidence highlight → context → payoff should reduce visual mismatch and improve the first major retention drop compared with static cards or generic B-roll. Keep the narration beat unchanged where possible so the visual change is measurable.
- **Edit rule:** every scene needs a purposeful visual reset, but zooms and caption motion must direct attention to evidence rather than act as decoration. Reject footage that is static, soft, unrelated, watermarked, or too cropped to identify the subject.
- **Roblox benchmark:** high-view gameplay openings begin inside the exact experience. Avatar direction is story-dependent; legibility of avatar, objective, native UI, and consequence matters more than always moving toward or away from camera. Prefer live mechanic proof, short emphasis captions, and readable midtones over generic Roblox montages.
